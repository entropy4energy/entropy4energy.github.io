The Entropy for Energy (S4E) Laboratory designs materials for energy technologies by controlling chemical disorder. Mixing many elements on one crystal lattice raises the configurational entropy, which can stabilize phases that are not stable in simpler compositions and creates a composition space far larger than can be searched experimentally. The group combines high-throughput first-principles calculations, machine-learned interatomic potentials, and curated databases to search that space, and works with experimental partners at Johns Hopkins University and beyond to make and test the candidates.

Since the group started at Johns Hopkins University in 2022, its work has followed three connected directions: hydrogen production from water and heat, materials for nuclear fission and fusion environments, and the data infrastructure that makes disordered materials tractable to computation. Results include the incorporation of iodine into pyrochlore ceramics for radionuclide waste forms and a descriptor-based search for platinum-free fuel-cell catalysts across more than 20,000 alloy compositions. A perspective from the group identifies reduced lattice thermal conductivity as a fifth core effect of high-entropy materials. The program is supported by ARPA-E, the Seaver Institute, the Department of the Air Force, and Johns Hopkins University institutes including ROSEI and the Data Science and AI Institute. Two provisional patents cover the group’s catalysts and waste forms.

The S4E lab is recruiting PhD students and postdoctoral researchers with backgrounds in materials science, physics, chemistry, and computer science. See the Jobs page for how to apply.
