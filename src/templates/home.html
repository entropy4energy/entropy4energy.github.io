The Entropy for Energy (S4E) Laboratory develops computational and AI methods to design chemically disordered materials for energy applications. Disordered materials can access an enormous range of compositions and atomic configurations, but only a small fraction can be explored experimentally. We develop models that predict and generate promising compositions and atomic structures, then synthesize and test them in our laboratory and with collaborators.

Since 2022, our work at Johns Hopkins University has focused on hydrogen production, materials for fission and fusion, and the computational methods needed to model and design chemical disorder. Iodine now substitutes into pyrochlore ceramics that classical crystal-chemistry rules exclude, a starting point for durable waste forms. Platinum-free fuel-cell catalysts emerged from a chemical space of more than 20,000 high-entropy alloys. A perspective from the group identifies reduced lattice thermal conductivity as a fifth core effect of high-entropy materials. The program is supported by ARPA-E, the Seaver Institute, the Department of the Air Force, and Johns Hopkins University institutes including ROSEI and the Data Science and AI Institute. Two provisional patents cover the group’s catalysts and waste forms.

The S4E lab is recruiting PhD students and postdoctoral researchers with backgrounds in materials science, physics, chemistry, and computer science. See the Jobs page for how to apply.
