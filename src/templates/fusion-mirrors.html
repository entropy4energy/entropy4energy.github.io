Reflectivity R is the percentage of incident light reflected. These calculations describe the optical response of ideal, uncoated materials.

Optical definitions

n and k denote the normal-z mode 1 refractive index and extinction coefficient. R is the unpolarized full-tensor reflectivity with Cartesian z as the surface normal. For anisotropic materials, this mode-specific n, k pair and the full-tensor R describe different optical quantities. Gaps indicate unsupported or invalid values.

Reading the plots

R (%) is the percentage of incident power reflected. n is the refractive index, and k is the extinction coefficient.

The curves describe calculated, uncoated surfaces at normal incidence. Line colors and patterns identify the selected materials or calculation datasets. Gaps indicate unsupported values.

Coatings in the study

The study combines literature optical constants with calculated dielectric tensors for 115 compositions (227 optical responses). Use the plots above to compare bare interfaces. Coating calculations use homogeneous layers on semi-infinite metal substrates.

The three-band score R3 is the equal-weight mean of reflectivity over 200–400, 400–700, and 700–1200 nm. The coating-induced change is ΔR3 = R3,coated − R3,bare, in percentage points. Negative values indicate lower reflectivity than the matching bare metal.

Across the sampled 0.1–500 nm thicknesses, SiO2 and Al2O3 coatings on Rh, Mo and W lower the three-band score in both the literature and standard PAW/PBE models. Below 5 nm, the optical penalty approaches zero. Interference makes the full thickness dependence nonmonotonic.

Coating order depends on wavelength. At 532 nm and 100 nm thickness, the standard PAW/PBE Al2O3 coatings have smaller losses than SiO2 on Rh, Mo and W, reversing their three-band order.

For 152 matched coated devices at 100 nm and normal incidence under unpolarized illumination, literature and standard PAW/PBE three-band scores differ by a mean absolute 10.64 percentage points.

Calculation details

Density functional theory (DFT) optical responses were calculated with VASP. The spectra use the 1 nm wavelength grid from the screening calculations.

The incident medium has unit refractive index and relative permeability, representing vacuum (labeled air in the study). The material is homogeneous and semi-infinite, with Cartesian z as the surface normal. Unpolarized R averages the total reflected powers for the two transverse incident polarizations, including polarization conversion.

The standard PAW dataset and density response match the study's main bare-interface calculation. The GW-oriented PAW option also uses PBE. The density and current response channels are kept separate.

This explorer reads the stored 1 nm grid. At off-grid wavelengths such as 656.3 nm, it reports the nearest stored wavelength and offset. The study's diagnostic-line calculations interpolate complex dielectric components before solving the reflected fields.
