CHAOS Terms of Use

Version 2026-12. Effective November 23, 2026. Wording revised November 23, 2026, with no change in substance.

Version 2026-12. Questions: s4e-chaos@jhu.edu.

CHAOS is a database of density functional theory calculations of oxides and alloys, developed by the Entropy for Energy Laboratory at Johns Hopkins University ("JHU"). These terms are an agreement between you and JHU. You accept them by checking the box when you ask for a CHAOS account, sign in, or ask for a key.

1. Accounts and approved purpose. Each account is assigned to one person for an approved research purpose, which you describe when you ask for the account. JHU approves each account, and may decline an application or restrict or terminate access at its discretion. An email address or affiliation, at Johns Hopkins or elsewhere, does not by itself give access or permission. Your access is personal to the approved purpose. You may not give CHAOS data, your account or your keys to anyone else, including colleagues at your own institution, except by publishing as section 5 allows. Each person who uses CHAOS data needs their own approved account, including the members of a research group named in an approval under section 4. A new purpose, or a material change to the approved one, needs new approval.

2. Research use is free. With an approved account, you may use CHAOS for the approved research or teaching at a university, college, non-profit research institute or government laboratory, including a national laboratory operated under contract with a government. You may publish results, figures and tables of values from CHAOS that support your work, with the citation in section 10. Company-funded research, and research whose results a company has committed to receive or use, is commercial use under section 6(a).

3. Research limits. An account may receive up to 50 disordered systems and 1,000 ordered structures in any 12 months. Repeated access to the same record does not count toward the limit again. Once you reach a limit, CHAOS will notify you and restrict access to additional records. JHU may raise these limits for an approved project, in writing, for the period it states. Higher limits granted for a project do not by themselves grant any permission under section 4.

4. Machine learning and AI models. Unless JHU has given you permission in writing, you may not use CHAOS data, in whole or in part, to:

(a) train, pre-train, fine-tune or adapt any machine-learning or artificial-intelligence model, including language models, interatomic potentials and property predictors;

(b) distill a model, or generate training data for a model, from CHAOS-Agent answers or from any other CHAOS output;

(c) build embeddings, vector stores, retrieval indexes or other collections used to ground, prompt or augment a model;

(d) evaluate, validate or benchmark a model on CHAOS data, other than as allowed in section 5(b); or

(e) give CHAOS data to another person or service so that they may do any of these things.

What data are covered. "CHAOS data" means values, records, files and text obtained, on or after the day you accept these terms, from the CHAOS API, from any CHAOS page or file shown after sign-in, and from CHAOS-Agent, and any copy, extract or derivative of them. It includes data read by you, by software you run, or by an AI agent acting for you. This section does not apply to a CHAOS release used under that release's license (section 8).

Training and model release. Each approval specifies the applicable access limits and the people who may work with the data and with any model trained on it; each of them needs their own account (section 1). Permission to train a model using CHAOS data does not include permission to release it. Releasing a trained model, its weights, or a related service requires separate written approval from JHU. This does not apply to a model trained only on data from a CHAOS release used under its own license.

After an approval ends. When your approval expires or ends, you must delete the CHAOS data obtained under that approval. The people named in the approval may keep a model trained under it for internal research and reproducibility. It may not be released, redistributed, offered as a service, or trained further on CHAOS data without renewed approval.

How to request permission. Ask at s4e-chaos@jhu.edu. JHU decides each request in writing, under the CHAOS data-use standard published at https://chaos.s4e.ai/terms, usually within 10 working days, and may refuse it. JHU charges no fee for permission given to universities, non-profit institutes and government laboratories for research. Training for or by a company, or for a product or service, is permitted only under a license from Johns Hopkins Technology Ventures. JHU does not permit the use of CHAOS data to train or improve general-purpose AI models.

5. Research, evaluation and training.

(a) Ordinary research. You may compare CHAOS values with your own calculations, experimental measurements or published results, and publish those comparisons, and figures and tables of CHAOS values that support your work, with the citation in section 10, within the limits in section 3.

(b) Model evaluation. You may compare the predictions of your own model with up to 20 CHAOS disordered systems or 200 CHAOS ordered structures without a request, provided those values were not used to train, tune or select the model. You may publish that comparison with the citation in section 10.

(c) Model training and larger evaluation. Training a model or conducting a larger evaluation using live CHAOS data requires written approval under section 4.

6. Other uses that need written permission from JHU.

(a) Commercial use: use by or for a company, or use in making, selling or supporting a product or service, including internal screening and consulting. Company-funded research, and research whose results a company has committed to receive or use, is commercial use under CHAOS's policy. JHU licenses commercial use through Johns Hopkins Technology Ventures.

(b) Receiving more than the research limits, or building a database, data service or website from CHAOS.

(c) Sharing CHAOS data, or anything made from them other than published results, with anyone.

Ask at s4e-chaos@jhu.edu.

7. How you may access CHAOS. You may access CHAOS data only through the CHAOS API with your own key, and through the CHAOS pages in an ordinary browser, within the published limits. You may not scrape, crawl or copy CHAOS pages by automated means; use several accounts or keys, or other people's, to raise the limits; or get around a limit, a sign-in or any other technical measure. Your agreement to this section is a condition of your access.

8. CHAOS releases. A CHAOS release is a fixed, versioned dataset that JHU publishes under its own license. That license governs the data contained in the release, regardless of where identical copies are obtained. The published release defines which data are covered by its license. New calculations, updated values and additional records in the live CHAOS service are not part of a release unless the release names them. These terms do not limit a release.

9. Use is recorded. JHU records each request with its account, key, address and the entries returned. JHU uses these records to operate CHAOS, to monitor and report its use, and to enforce these terms.

10. Citation. Cite CHAOS as: CHAOS, Entropy for Energy Laboratory, Johns Hopkins University, https://chaos.s4e.ai, and the paper that describes it once published.

11. No warranty. CHAOS is provided as is. Calculated values may contain errors. JHU is not liable for any use of CHAOS.

12. Breach. If you use CHAOS data in breach of section 1, 3, 4, 6 or 7:

(a) JHU may suspend or close your accounts at once;

(b) you must delete the CHAOS data you hold and any model, embedding or index made with them in breach, and confirm the deletion in writing within 30 days of JHU's request; and

(c) you agree that money alone would not repair the harm, and that JHU may seek an injunction.

Sections 1, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 12 continue to apply to CHAOS data you obtained while you had an account, after the account ends.

13. Changes. JHU will email account holders before changing these terms. A new version applies once you accept it. CHAOS will request acceptance when you sign in or request an API key.

14. Earlier licenses. Data obtained before acceptance of these terms remain subject to the license under which they were provided. These terms govern subsequent access to and use of controlled CHAOS data, except for separately licensed CHAOS releases under section 8.

15. Law. These terms are governed by the laws of the State of Maryland.

CHAOS data-use standard

How requests are reviewed. We review requests under section 4 of the terms based on the proposed research, the amount of data needed, whether the proposed output would reproduce or substitute for the CHAOS database, and who else will have access to the data or the trained model. Commercial requests, including research a company funds or has committed to receive, are handled through Johns Hopkins Technology Ventures. We usually respond within 10 working days. A refusal gives its reason and can be appealed once, within 30 days.

Standard conditions of an approval

  1. Cite CHAOS as section 10 of the terms gives it, and name CHAOS and the date of the approval in the methods and the data availability statement.
  2. Using CHAOS does not require co-authorship.
  3. The data stay with the people named in the approval. Published training, validation and benchmark sets may list CHAOS identifiers, but not the underlying controlled data.
  4. Model weights are released only with a separate written permission, under a non-commercial license that names CHAOS.
  5. Send a link to each preprint, paper, repository or model page within 30 days of it becoming public.
  6. Each approval states its last day, normally 12 months after approval, and the access limits that apply to it. You may request renewal by email.
  7. At the end of the approval, delete the CHAOS data and confirm the deletion by email. The people named in the approval may keep a model trained under it for internal research and reproducibility. It may not be released, redistributed, offered as a service, or trained further on CHAOS data without renewed approval.
  8. The approval covers the people and the use named in the request. Additional researchers or changes in the approved purpose require a new request.
